# Group Admission and Entry Payments Runbook

GT-02B2 replaces legacy direct join and manual payment-confirmation paths. Invitations snapshot the effective entry-requirement version, including its capacity limit. Acceptance creates an applicant; it does not grant membership. Admission requires an approved `membership_action` proposal with `action=admit`, the membership identifier, and the applicant in the direct-conflict list. Execution rechecks tenant, group, constitution, snapshotted capacity, identity tier, membership version, and decision evidence.

An approved zero-fee applicant becomes active immediately. A fee-bearing applicant moves to `pending_payment` and may initialize only the exact amount and currency stored on the snapshotted rule. Activation accepts only a succeeded payment owned by the same tenant, membership, and payer with a success journal. It then posts a balanced allocation from customer funds to group membership-fee income and records immutable membership evidence. Provider references alone are never proof of payment.

Initialize a due entry payment with `POST /api/payments/initialize-group`, an `Idempotency-Key` header, and a body containing the UUID fields `group_id` and `member_id`. The organization and payer are always taken from the authenticated tenant context; client-supplied tenant, payer, amount, currency, or provider ownership fields are ignored.

If initialization times out, retry the same command with the same idempotency key. If the provider is uncertain, query or process its signed webhook through the payment engine; never edit membership payment fields. A provider reversal posts both the inbound reversal and the membership-fee allocation reversal, marks the payment condition failed, preserves membership and governance history, and removes the member from new paid-member voting eligibility until remediation. Membership-fee refunds are blocked from the generic refund endpoint and must use the later approved exit/refund workflow.

During an incident, disable `groups.membership.manage` to stop invitations, admission execution, and new membership payments; also disable `financial.payments.accept_new` when acquisition must stop. Existing provider callbacks and reconciliation remain serviceable through their servicing flags. Before live use the migration may be rolled back. After live use preserve requirements, proposals, payments, allocations, journals, and events; use compensating commands rather than deletion or direct updates.
